Projects

What is in front of the street.

Some of these are projects, with a site and a budget in play. Some are problems neighbors have named out loud and nobody has scheduled. The label says which is which.

How to read this

Four labels, meaning what they say.

A label is not a promise, and it is not the Borough's work program. Most of this list is at the stage of having been said in a public meeting.

The list

Everything currently on the plate.

  • Public restrooms

    Next up

    Creek Street has spent years sending people elsewhere for a bathroom. As of January 2026, KRBD reported six public restrooms downtown, four of them open in summer only, with the closest to the street inside the Tongass Historical Museum and across the street from the Salvation Army thrift store. The city's public works department had drafted locations in the Creek Street area. The joint city–borough committee agreed to price two of them: one near Dolly's House on Creek Street, and one on Stedman Street between New York Cafe and Ketchikan Dry Goods. The borough assembly that month approved a $500,000 placeholder in the coming fiscal year's budget. KRBD reported that construction would be paid from cruise-passenger funds, and that how the placeholder would be used had not been settled.

    Later in the year, at the August 17 public meeting of the design review board, members named restrooms the near-term priority and expected a September meeting to take them up after outreach to City Public Works. They also said placement matters: the boardwalk already has pinch points, and a new building has to be considered against those.

    KRBD, January 16, 2026. And the August 17, 2026 note — draft minutes, not yet adopted. This site does not have a later decision.

  • Trees in danger of falling

    Named

    Members of the design review board identified hazard trees as a safety risk to Creek Street buildings. No project is scheduled here.

    From the August 17, 2026 note — draft minutes, not yet adopted.

  • The entrance to Creek Street

    Named

    The visitor entrance and the approach to it were described as dangerous where pedestrians and vehicle traffic combine.

    From the August 17, 2026 note — draft minutes, not yet adopted.

  • Congestion on the boardwalk

    Named

    Pedestrian congestion during the tourism season, with storeowners asking visitors to wait outside at capacity. The boardwalk's current width may already be doing useful work regulating flow. Widening sections of it could increase congestion rather than relieve it.

    From the August 17, 2026 note — draft minutes, not yet adopted.

  • A neighborhood plan for the historic district

    Named

    Members said the district needs one. In public comment the same day, the Borough transit director said his department will work with the Planning Department and Planning Commission toward an updated area plan. More than one body is now pointing at the same gap.

    From the August 17, 2026 note — draft minutes, not yet adopted.

  • Flooding at high tide and in heavy rain

    Named

    Named among the district's standing problems. No project is attached to it on this site.

    From the August 17, 2026 note — draft minutes, not yet adopted.

  • Spreading visitors around the community

    Broader than the street

    Dispersal came up as part of the larger picture. It is wider than any one building on the boardwalk, and it shapes every other item on this list.

    From the August 17, 2026 note — draft minutes, not yet adopted.

  • Bears on the creek

    Unsettled

    The boardwalk runs along a salmon stream, so bears are neighbors here. At the August meeting, members asked whether bear control could fall within the design review board's purview. That question has not been answered. It remains a street problem without an owner on this list.

    From the August 17, 2026 note — draft minutes, not yet adopted.
