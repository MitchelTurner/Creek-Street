Visiting Creek Street

        
Practical information for an afternoon on the boardwalk.

      

      

        

          

            
Getting there
Enter from Stedman Street under the Creek Street sign. It's a short walk from the downtown cruise docks.

            
Getting around
The main boardwalk is reached from street level at Stedman Street. Married Man's Trail and some side connections use stairs, and the planks can be slick when wet.

            
Up the hill
The Cape Fox Lodge tram climbs from the boardwalk to the lodge and its restaurants. Check with the lodge for current hours.

            
Restrooms
Their status is under public discussion. See the Development page.

          

          

            When to go
            
Mornings and evenings are quieter than midday on multi-ship days. Check that day's cruise passenger count at ktnport.com.

History

        
From houses on pilings to one of Southeast Alaska's most photographed streets.

      

      
  1. 1902–1903
    A city ordinance moves brothels out of downtown to the east side of Ketchikan Creek. Houses go up on pilings, linked by a boardwalk, and Creek Street becomes the red-light district.
  2. 1903
    The Star, at 5 Creek Street, starts as a small gable-roofed house. It is enlarged in 1910 and 1917.
  3. 1906
    The house at 24 Creek Street, later Dolly's House, is standing by this year.
  4. 1919
    Dolly Arthur moves into No. 24, where she lives for more than fifty years.
  5. 1924
    The New York Hotel is built on Stedman Street at the foot of Creek Street.
  6. Early 1940s
    Coast Guard pressure forces a wartime closure of the houses in the interest of national defense.
  7. 1953–1954
    Prostitution is outlawed and the red-light era on Creek Street ends.
  8. 1972
    A fire at The Star leaves the building derelict.
  9. 1973
    Dolly Arthur leaves No. 24. The house later opens as a museum of her life.
  10. 1989
    A replica of the Chief Johnson totem pole, carved by Israel Shotridge, is raised.
  11. 1991
    The Star is restored, and the tram between Creek Street and the new Cape Fox Lodge begins service.
  12. 1993
    The Star is listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
  13. 1996
    The Stedman–Thomas Historic District, including the New York Hotel, is listed on the National Register.
  14. 2014
    The Creek Street Historic District, covering Creek Street, Married Man's Trail and Totem Way, is listed on the National Register on August 6.
  15. 2025
    A rebuilt tram, with a new stop at the Eagle's Nest restaurant, is certified in June and celebrated in September.
  16. 2026
    Public design review for the Creek Street district resumes after about ten years.

      
The street's character comes from its buildings, railings, signs and pilings working together.

    


    

      

        
Common questions

      

      
Where is Creek Street?
In downtown Ketchikan, Alaska, along Ketchikan Creek. The main entrance is on Stedman Street, a short walk from the downtown cruise docks.
What is Creek Street known for?
Its wooden boardwalk and houses built on pilings over Ketchikan Creek, and its past as Ketchikan's red-light district from the early 1900s until the 1950s. The street is listed on the National Register of Historic Places as the Creek Street Historic District.
Does it cost anything to visit?
Walking the boardwalk is free. Museums, the tram and shops along the way may charge for admission or rides.
When can you see salmon in Ketchikan Creek?
Pink salmon run in August, chum from late August into early September, and coho from late August into October. Timing varies from year to year.
What is Dolly's House?
A museum at 24 Creek Street in the house where Dolly Arthur lived from 1919 to 1973, furnished with her belongings from those decades.
How do you get from Creek Street to Cape Fox Lodge?
Ride the tram from its landing on the boardwalk, or climb Married Man's Trail, about ten minutes on foot.
How much time should I plan?
About an hour to walk the boardwalk and watch the creek. Allow more if you visit the museums or ride the tram up the hill.
