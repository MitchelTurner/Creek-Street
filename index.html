The street

A boardwalk over the creek, still in use.

The historic district takes in Creek Street, Married Man's Trail, and Totem Way. Wood-frame buildings stand on pilings, and the way through them is a pedestrian boardwalk. The district was listed on the National Register of Historic Places on August 6, 2014.

Change is the normal condition

The street was never frozen. Buildings here have been rebuilt, repainted, and repurposed for a century. The character of the place depends on those ordinary changes landing well.

It reads as one thing

No building on Creek Street stands alone. Rooflines, deck heights, sign scale, and color carry across neighbors. A change on one lot shows up in the boardwalk it joins.

More than the buildings

Restrooms, hazard trees, the entrance, crowding in summer, water at high tide. Those shape a day on the street as much as a railing does. They are tracked with the projects.

Design review is the Borough's

Exterior work in the historic district goes to the Borough's architectural design review board. Members, meetings, and how review works are on the Borough's page. This site does not speak for that board.

Coming up

What is actually in front of the street.

A short list. The full one says where each item stands, and which parts are not a Creek Street project at all.

  • Next up Public restrooms The city and the borough have been studying sites. Where they go matters as much as whether they go in.
  • Named The entrance Pedestrians and vehicles mix at the approach. People who use the street have called it dangerous.
  • Named A neighborhood plan The district does not have a current area plan. More than one public body has said it needs one.
  • Named The boardwalk at capacity Summer crowds bunch up. Widening the deck is not an obvious fix — it may move more people through, not fewer.
