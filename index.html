Why this board exists

Creek Street is still a working street, not a museum piece.

People live and do business on the boardwalk. Roofs fail, decks rot, signs change, businesses turn over. The district's character survives when those ordinary changes are made with some shared sense of what the place is — not when change stops. That's the whole job of this board.

Change is expected

The district was never frozen. Buildings here have been rebuilt, repainted, and repurposed for a century. We're here to help changes land well, not to prevent them.

The street is one object

No building on Creek Street reads alone. Rooflines, deck heights, sign scale, and color all carry across neighbors. We look at a proposal in the context of the boardwalk it joins.

Advice, not a permit

We're an advisory board. We give guidance to the Planning Commission and the Zoning Administrator on proposals referred to us. Coming to us early costs nothing and usually saves time later.

The record stays public

Agendas, what we discussed, and what we recommended get posted here after each meeting. If you want to know why something was decided, you should be able to find out.

Scope

What comes to us — and what doesn't

If you're not sure which side your project falls on, ask. A two-minute question is cheaper than a redesign.

We review

Within the Historic District zone

  • Exterior alterations visible from the boardwalk or the creek
  • New construction and additions
  • Rooflines, dormers, porches, railings, and stairs
  • Windows and doors — openings, proportions, materials
  • Signs, awnings, and exterior lighting
  • Decking, pilings, and structural repair that changes appearance
  • Exterior paint and cladding materials

We don't review

Handled elsewhere or not at all

  • Interiors
  • Ordinary maintenance and in-kind repair
  • Building code, life safety, and structural permitting
  • Land use, zoning, and variance decisions
  • What a business sells or how it operates
  • Anything outside the Historic District boundary

Attend

Anyone can come. You don't need a project.

Meetings are open to the public and held in the Assembly Chambers. Property owners, tenants, neighbors, and anyone who just wants to know what's being proposed on the boardwalk are all welcome to sit in and speak.

Sit in on a meeting

Show up a few minutes early. Agendas are posted here ahead of each meeting, and there's time set aside for public comment. If you'd like a reminder before the next one, send us your email and we'll add you to the notice list.

Bring a project

Have a change in mind for a building in the district? Get in touch before you're deep into drawings. Photos and a rough sketch are enough to start — we'd rather talk early than react late.
